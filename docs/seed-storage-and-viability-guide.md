

\# Seed Storage and Viability Guide



\## Purpose



This guide describes practical ways to label, store and monitor seeds

before sowing. Storage requirements vary by crop, seed variety,

packaging and local climate. Follow supplier instructions when available.



\## 1. Before storing seeds



\- Confirm the crop, variety, supplier and purchase date.

\- Keep the original lot or batch number where available.

\- Inspect packaging for moisture, tears, pests or visible mould.

\- Separate damaged or questionable lots from healthy stock.

\- Do not mix varieties or batches in the same unlabelled container.



\## 2. Storage conditions



\- Keep seed containers in a clean, dry and well-maintained location.

\- Protect seeds from direct sunlight, rain, floor moisture and pests.

\- Use suitable sealed packaging only when seed moisture is appropriate.

\- Avoid repeated movement between warm and cold environments that

&#x20; can cause condensation.

\- Monitor for leaks, dampness, insects and damaged containers.

\- Follow crop-specific temperature and humidity recommendations.

&#x20; There is no single ideal storage setting for every seed.



\## 3. Seed inventory label



Record the following for each lot:



| Field | What to record |

|---|---|

| Crop | Crop name |

| Variety | Variety or hybrid name |

| Lot number | Supplier batch identifier |

| Purchase date | Date received |

| Quantity | Quantity and unit |

| Storage location | Room, cabinet or container |

| Expiry or retest date | As stated by supplier or testing plan |

| Inspection date | Most recent visual inspection |

| Notes | Damage, moisture or other observations |



\## 4. Checking germination



A simple germination check can help assess a seed lot before sowing.

Use a representative sample and the recommended method for that crop.



1\. Select seeds randomly from the lot.

2\. Place a known number under suitable crop-specific test conditions.

3\. Keep the test medium and moisture appropriate for the crop.

4\. Count normal seedlings according to the relevant testing guidance.

5\. Record the number tested, normal seedlings, test date and method.



A basic percentage calculation is:



Germination percentage =

(normal seedlings counted / seeds tested) x 100



For example, if 80 normal seedlings emerge from 100 seeds tested,

the observed germination percentage is 80%.



This small check is only an estimate. Official seed certification,

laboratory testing and crop-specific standards may use more detailed

procedures.



\## 5. Stock rotation



\- Use supplier expiry dates and retest recommendations.

\- Review older lots before opening newly received stock.

\- Do not assume every older lot is unusable or every newer lot is viable.

\- Retest when appropriate, particularly after unsuitable storage.

\- Dispose of mouldy or pest-infested material responsibly and safely.



\## 6. Important precautions



\- Do not eat treated seeds.

\- Keep seed-treatment chemicals away from food and animal feed.

\- Follow product labels and local handling requirements.

\- Do not use germination results alone to guarantee field emergence.

\- Seek crop-specific advice when storage history or seed quality is uncertain.



\## Suggested review checklist



\- \[ ] Seed lot is identified and labelled.

\- \[ ] Packaging is intact and dry.

\- \[ ] Storage location is clean and suitable.

\- \[ ] Inspection and retest dates are recorded.

\- \[ ] Older lots have been reviewed.

\- \[ ] Germination results are recorded when testing is performed.
